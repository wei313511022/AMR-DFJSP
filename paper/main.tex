\title{When Idealized Travel Times Misrank Multi-Robot Schedules:
Execution-Grounded Learning for Capacitated Pickup and Delivery}

\begin{abstract}
Learned dispatchers often evaluate robot transport with fixed travel times,
although deployed robots share paths and exclusive stations. We study offline
capacitated multi-robot pickup and delivery by applying an idealized decoder
and a fixed collision-aware executor to identical complete schedules. On 100
instances with 60 jobs and 16 robots, a fixed 17-generator comparison set
gives a mean per-instance Kendall $\tau_b$ of $0.334\!\pm\!0.026$, a
$33.0\!\pm\!1.3\%$ inversion rate, and $29.8\!\pm\!3.7\%$ selection regret.
We then train a constructive graph policy with PPO from executor-evaluated
terminal makespan while a calibrated event-based surrogate advances partial
schedules and exposes projected station queues, committed workload, local
robot density, and action-specific waiting. At 60 jobs the surrogate raises
$\tau_b$ from $0.446$ to $0.730$, lowers selection regret from $4.1\%$ to
$1.2\%$, and scores a schedule about $290\times$ faster than the executor. A
matched $2\times2$ factorial shows that executor-grounded training reduces
single-rollout makespan by $15.9\%$ relative to the fully observed
idealized-objective arm; congestion observability adds a further $3.0\%$.
With eight executor-scored policy rollouts, the three-seed policy improves
makespan by $5.9\%$ over the test-set best fixed dispatching rule and by
$3.6\%$ over a genetic baseline. The results separate inexpensive congestion
projection during construction from the executor that defines solution value.
\end{abstract}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
This paper formulates offline capacitated multi-robot pickup and delivery
with explicit station contention and evaluation by a fixed
collision-aware executor. It distinguishes the value error of an idealized
transport model from its more consequential decision error---changes in the
ordering of complete schedules---and trains a constructive policy from
executor-evaluated terminal cost while using a calibrated surrogate for
intermediate states. The controlled evaluation
separates improvements due to congestion observability from those due to the
training objective. The idealized model substantially misranks schedules once
the candidate set includes policies optimized against it: at the 60-job
training workload, $38.4\%$ of comparable pairs are inverted and trusting its
selected schedule loses $30.6\%$ under execution. Executor grounding is the dominant
causal factor, while projected congestion adds a smaller effect that grows
with workload. The calibrated surrogate recovers much of the executor's
ranking at roughly $1/250$ of its scoring time. On the fixed layout, the
executor-scored eight-rollout policy improves on strong rule and genetic
baselines, although single-rollout generalization weakens away from its
60-job training distribution. Extending the study to other layouts, fleets,
and complete multi-agent routers is the principal direction for future work.
