\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

\IEEEPARstart{F}{leets} of autonomous mobile robots (AMRs) increasingly
perform the internal transfer of unit loads in transfer halls and terminals:
freight arriving at a bank of inbound doors is carried across a shared floor
and consolidated at outbound stations. The operator must decide which robot
carries which load, and in which order --- an assignment and sequencing
problem whose quality is not realised on a Gantt chart but on a physical
floor, where robots contend for exclusive service points and must never
occupy the same cell at the same instant.

Constructive deep-reinforcement-learning dispatchers have become the
standard learned approach to the scheduling half of this problem. Beginning
with L2D~\cite{zhang2020l2d}, such methods encode the disjunctive graph with
a graph neural network~\cite{xu2019gin}, commit one operation per decision
epoch, and generalise across instance sizes~\cite{song2023fjsp,%
smit2024gnnsurvey}. Transport-aware variants are now mature, spanning
composite operation--machine--vehicle actions over heterogeneous graphs,
graph isomorphism encoders trained with proximal policy optimisation, and
transformer encoders with dual-agent decompositions~\cite{moon2024hgs,%
yuan2026gin,yang2026cosched,matrans2026}, and continuing a line that
originates with the simultaneous machine-and-vehicle models of Bilge and
Ulusoy~\cite{bilge1995time,ulusoy1997ga}.

The realised time to transfer a load is the sum of three terms: free-space
travel, the excess incurred when collision-free routing forces a detour or a
wait, and the time spent queueing for an exclusive service point. Existing
formulations may be partitioned by which of the three they retain.

The learned-dispatching line retains only the first, representing vehicle
motion by a fixed travel-time matrix and treating handover as immediate ---
an idealisation inherited from the founding simultaneous
formulations~\cite{bilge1995time,ulusoy1997ga} and largely unexamined since.
A separate literature on integrated scheduling with conflict-free routing in
container terminals and flexible manufacturing systems recovers the second,
planning routes exactly on a guide-path
network~\cite{zhong2020integrated,li2024hierarchical,miyamoto2016local,%
nishida2023jit}. The third, however, is discharged by assumption even there:
loading and unloading are taken to begin the moment a vehicle arrives, so
that service points impose no queue~\cite{nishida2023jit}. Waiting is
therefore modelled where vehicles meet in transit and excluded where they
meet at a server --- which, as we show, is where most of it occurs.

The omission is usually justified as a second-order correction. We show that
in a dense AMR fleet it is neither second-order nor uniformly distributed.
On an unobstructed floor --- the configuration least favourable to the
argument, since free-space distance is there most nearly exact --- the
omitted terms account for a substantial fraction of realised makespan, and
that fraction grows monotonically with the ratio of robots to service
points.

Its composition matters more than its magnitude. Decomposing the gap using
the executor's own accounting shows that the majority is service-point
queueing rather than interference in transit, and that the queueing share
\emph{increases} with density. The distinction determines the appropriate
response: excess travel is a routing cost, remediable by a stronger path
planner, whereas queueing at an exclusive server is a scheduling cost, since
which robot is dispatched to which door, and in what order, fixes who waits
behind whom. The dominant error in the standard abstraction therefore lies
in precisely the quantity an assignment policy governs.

Correcting the training signal is not merely a matter of substituting a more
accurate simulator. Because the routing of an early leg depends on
reservations created by legs not yet committed, the makespan is defined only
on \emph{complete} solutions, and no consistent evaluation of a partial
sequence exists. The dense lower-bound-difference rewards that drive
constructive shop dispatchers~\cite{zhang2020l2d} are therefore unavailable
in principle, and credit must instead be assigned by rollout --- which we
ground in the executor itself.

Multi-agent path finding and its lifelong pickup-and-delivery variants form
a third group, computing collision-free space--time trajectories for online
task streams in warehouse grids~\cite{stern2019mapf,ma2017lifelong}, more
recently with learned components inside the planner~\cite{rlrhpp2026}. These
methods, including capacitated variants, model transport faithfully but take
the assignment as given. Within that setting Noda~\cite{noda2026deferral}
observes that immediate commitment can degrade service through congestion
and reservation interactions, and defers assignment by a tuned threshold: an
online heuristic that exploits the interaction without quantifying it. The
integrated methods enforce routing feasibility exactly but re-solve each
instance by search, learning no reusable policy. What remains unaddressed is
a constructive scheduling policy whose learning signal is the makespan
realised under the router that will execute it.

We claim no architectural novelty. The policy is a graph encoder with a
composite operation--robot action, as in~\cite{song2023fjsp,moon2024hgs,%
yuan2026gin}; our contention is that in this setting the training signal,
not the encoder, determines performance, and we test that claim directly.
The contributions are as follows.

\begin{itemize}
\item A formulation of AMR transfer with capacitated consolidation and
  exclusive service points as simulation-based optimisation against a
  collision-aware executor, together with an \emph{execution penalty}
  (Definition~\ref{def:penalty}) defined against the idealised transport
  model that the learned-dispatching literature assumes
  (Section~\ref{sec:problem}).
\item An empirical characterisation of that penalty: its magnitude, its
  monotone growth in the contention ratio $\eta$, and its decomposition into
  queueing and routing components, establishing that the queueing term
  dominates and that its share increases with density
  (Section~\ref{sec:experiments}).
\item An execution-grounded training scheme in which the advantage of each
  committed decision is obtained by completing the prefix under a
  dispatching rule and executing both variants under the collision-aware
  executor, together with a surrogate transition model calibrated offline
  against that executor (Sections~\ref{sec:calibration},
  \ref{sec:training}).
\item An evaluation against tuned dispatching rules, a consolidation
  baseline and a metaheuristic at matched computational budget; and a
  factorial ablation over training signal (idealised versus executor-%
  grounded) and state observability (with and without congestion features),
  separating what the policy is shown from what it is rewarded for
  (Section~\ref{sec:experiments}).
\end{itemize}

The remainder of the paper is organised as follows.
Section~\ref{sec:related} reviews learned dispatching, multi-agent path
finding, and integrated scheduling with conflict-free routing.
Section~\ref{sec:problem} states the problem and defines the execution
penalty. Sections~\ref{sec:architecture}--\ref{sec:training} describe the
policy, its calibrated surrogate, and the training scheme.
Section~\ref{sec:experiments} reports results, and
Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} concludes.
