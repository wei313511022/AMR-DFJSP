\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Learned Constructive Scheduling}

L2D~\cite{zhang2020l2d} introduced a graph-based constructive policy for job
shops, using the change in a makespan lower bound as a dense reward. Flexible
job-shop extensions use heterogeneous operation--machine graphs and composite
actions~\cite{song2023fjsp}; recent work adds vehicle nodes, hierarchical
operation--machine--vehicle decisions, and transformer or multi-agent
encoders~\cite{moon2024hgs,yuan2026gin,yang2026cosched,matrans2026}. These
developments establish the encoder and composite action as mature design
choices for the evaluator-focused study developed here.

The cited transport-aware schedulers principally represent motion with fixed
travel times between resources. This supports inexpensive partial-schedule
evaluation but does not reproduce delays from shared-space reservations or
exclusive pickup and delivery service. Our comparison therefore concerns the
training evaluator rather than the representational capacity of the encoder.

\subsection{Integrated Assignment and Conflict-Free Routing}

Scheduling and material handling have long been recognised as coupled
decisions~\cite{bilge1995time,ulusoy1997ga}. Conflict-aware methods integrate
dispatch and route search for capacitated AGVs~\cite{miyamoto2016local},
container-terminal operations~\cite{zhong2020integrated}, and just-in-time
delivery~\cite{nishida2023jit}. Integrated MAPD likewise uses routed delivery
cost to inform assignment and includes a multi-load variant
\cite{chen2021integrated}. These methods optimise routing-aware solutions per
instance rather than learning the constructive policy considered here.

Learning-based integration is also established. Li \emph{et al.}
\cite{li2024hierarchical} train a dynamic task-assignment policy whose action
rewards are evaluated by a lower-level conflict-free path generator. Wang
\emph{et al.}~\cite{wang2026collaborative} jointly train task-allocation and
path-planning agents, while a reinforcement-learning hyper-heuristic combines
task sequencing with conflict-avoidance rules but re-runs search for each
instance~\cite{li2024rlhh}. Building on this established integration of
learning, assignment, and routing, we examine complete offline
pickup--delivery sequences under a fixed deployment executor. This controlled
setting makes evaluator-induced value and ranking errors measurable on a
common schedule set.

\subsection{MAPF, Commitment, and Service Resources}

Multi-agent path finding computes collision-free space--time trajectories on
shared graphs~\cite{stern2019mapf}; lifelong MAPD combines repeated routing
with online task service~\cite{ma2017lifelong}. Learned components also appear
inside the planner, for example as priority policies for rolling-horizon
prioritised planning~\cite{rlrhpp2026}. These works provide the routing
foundations used by the deployment executor but optimise a different decision
layer from an offline constructive schedule.

Assignment itself is not fixed throughout the MAPD literature. Integrated
methods couple assignment and routing~\cite{chen2021integrated}, and deliberate
assignment deferral shows that commitment timing changes future reservations
and deadline performance~\cite{noda2026deferral}. Service resources have also
been modelled explicitly: recent multi-subsystem MAPD work coordinates
single-dock handover stations with finite buffers~\cite{zang2026buffers}.
These results support, rather than constitute, our motivating observation that
assignment, routing, and service contention interact. The present paper asks
how this interaction biases an idealised offline training objective and how to
assign its terminal cost to individual commitments.

\subsection{Credit Assignment from Terminal Cost}

Policy-gradient methods can train directly from a terminal return
\cite{williams1992reinforce}, but baselines are commonly used to reduce
variance. Self-critical sequence training compares a sampled sequence with a
greedy decode~\cite{rennie2017self}; neural routing methods similarly evaluate
policy and rollout completions~\cite{kool2019attention}. Our estimator belongs
to this family. Its distinguishing construction is paired and
prefix-conditioned: the policy commitment and its baseline alternative are
completed by the same rule and both complete schedules are evaluated by the
unchanged deployment executor. This pairing supplies action-level credit
while preserving the executor-relative terminal objective.

\subsection{Position of This Work}

This paper focuses on the intersection of five elements: an offline complete
schedule, class-substitutable robot capacity, non-preemptive exclusive service
with finite waiting space, collision-aware execution by a fixed planner, and
paired prefix-conditioned terminal evaluation. Its empirical contribution is
to determine when the idealised transport model changes not only schedule
value but also schedule ordering. Its methodological contribution is the
paired estimator used to train against that executor-relative objective.
