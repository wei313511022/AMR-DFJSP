\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Learning to Dispatch for Shop Scheduling}

Constructive deep-reinforcement-learning dispatchers for shop
scheduling originate with L2D \cite{zhang2020l2d}, which encodes the
job-shop disjunctive graph with a GIN \cite{xu2019gin}, dispatches one
eligible operation per step, and trains with PPO on a dense
lower-bound-difference reward; the learned policy is size-agnostic and
outperforms priority dispatching rules. Subsequent work extends the
paradigm to flexible job shops with heterogeneous operation--machine
graphs and joint operation--machine actions \cite{song2023fjsp}, and a
recent survey traces this line \cite{smit2024gnnsurvey}. These methods
schedule abstract machines: material transport, vehicles, and floor
space are absent from both model and evaluation. Our graph
construction inherits the adding-arc scheme of this lineage, but our
policy additionally assigns the transporting robot, and its training
signal is grounded in physically executed schedules rather than graph
lower bounds.

\subsection{Scheduling with Transportation (FJSP-T)}

Integrating material handling into shop scheduling dates to the
simultaneous machine-and-AGV formulation of Bilge and Ulusoy
\cite{bilge1995time,ulusoy1997ga}, followed by decades of exact and
metaheuristic approaches. Recent deep-learning methods address the
same formulation end to end: notably, the heterogeneous graph
scheduler of Moon \emph{et al.} \cite{moon2024hgs} models operations,
machines, and vehicles as a heterogeneous graph and selects composite
(operation, machine, vehicle) actions, an action structure our actor
shares. Throughout this line---classical and learned
alike---transportation is reduced to a fixed travel-time matrix;
\cite{moon2024hgs} assumes identical loaded travel times for all
vehicles between any machine pair. Collision avoidance, congestion,
and queueing at handover points, which dominate dense robotic
terminals, are outside these models.

\subsection{Multi-Agent Path Finding and Pickup--Delivery}

The complementary literature solves transport with full physical
rigor. Multi-agent path finding (MAPF) computes collision-free
space--time trajectories \cite{stern2019mapf}, and its lifelong
pickup-and-delivery variant (MAPD) serves online task streams in
warehouse grids \cite{ma2017lifelong}, with recent learning-guided
planners improving scalability. The task model, however, is
deliberately thin: one agent serves one task, and neither
single-server handover resources with service durations nor
capacitated load consolidation is represented---precisely the
structure that makes cross-docking a scheduling problem.

\subsection{Integrated Scheduling and Conflict-Free Routing}

A smaller line, largely in automated container terminals and flexible
manufacturing, couples task scheduling with conflict-free vehicle
routing \cite{zhong2020integrated,hierarchical2024agv,
nishi2015capacitated}. These approaches decompose hierarchically and
re-solve per instance with search or metaheuristics; the routing
feasibility is enforced, but no constructive policy is learned, and
solution times grow with instance size. To our knowledge, no prior
method trains a constructive scheduling policy whose learning signal
is the makespan realized under collision-aware execution, nor
addresses the dock-exclusive, capacity-coupled structure of AMR-served
cross-docking. This work occupies that intersection: rich scheduling
structure from the FJSP-T line, physical execution from the MAPF line,
and end-to-end learning from the dispatching line.
