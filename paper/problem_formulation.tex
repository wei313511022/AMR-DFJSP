\subsection{System and Schedule}
\label{sec:system}

The workspace is a four-connected grid $\mathcal W$ with Manhattan distance
$\Delta$. A fleet $\mathcal M$ of $m$ unit-speed AMRs serves inbound doors
$\mathcal D^{\mathrm{in}}$ and outbound stations
$\mathcal D^{\mathrm{out}}$; Fig.~\ref{fig:layout} shows the controlled
instance family. Each service point $d$ is an exclusive, non-preemptive
server with a finite set $W_d$ of adjacent waiting cells. Contention is swept
through the fleet-to-inbound-server ratio
$\eta=m/|\mathcal D^{\mathrm{in}}|$, with layout and workload fixed.

Each parcel $j\in\mathcal J=\{1,\ldots,n\}$ has size
$\sigma_j\in\mathcal S=\{A,B,C\}$, service time $p_j$, origin $o_j$, and
destination $d_j$. It requires pickup $\rho_j$ followed by delivery
$\delta_j$ by one AMR. Robot $k$ has $Q_s$ rack bays of class $s$; a parcel
may use a bay of its own class or a larger class. With $N_s^k(t)$ denoting its
onboard class-$s$ load, capacity is equivalent to
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{s\succeq\varsigma}N_s^k(t)
 \le \sum_{s\succeq\varsigma}Q_s,
 \quad \forall k,\ \varsigma\in\mathcal S,\ t\ge0.
 \label{eq:nested}
\end{equation}

A schedule $(\bm a,\pi)$ assigns each parcel to one AMR through
$\bm a\in\mathcal M^n$ and orders all $2n$ operations through $\pi$. For an
event $e$ of parcel $j(e)$, let $s_e,c_e$, and $\ell(e)$ denote its start,
completion, and location; let $\mathcal E_d$ be the events at server $d$.
Feasibility requires
\begin{align}
 c_e &= s_e+p_{j(e)},                                      \label{eq:nonpreempt}\\
 s_{\delta_j} &\ge c_{\rho_j},                             \label{eq:precedence}\\
 [s_e,c_e)\cap[s_{e'},c_{e'})&=\emptyset,
 && e,e'\in\mathcal E_d,                                  \label{eq:exclusive}\\
 s_v &\ge c_u+t_k(\ell(u),\ell(v)),                       \label{eq:travel}\\
 t_k(x,y)&\ge\Delta(x,y), && x,y\in\mathcal W .          \label{eq:route-lb}
\end{align}
Equation~\eqref{eq:nested} and pickup--delivery precedence also hold along
each robot sequence. Every AMR starts at its dedicated bay $b_k$ and returns
there after completing its work.

\subsection{Executor-Relative Objective}
\label{sec:execution}

A fixed executor $\Phi$ routes each leg by space--time $\mathrm A^*$ over a
shared reservation table, enforces the queues $W_d$, and returns
\begin{equation}
 \Phi(\bm a,\pi)=
 \big(C_{\max}^{\Phi}(\bm a,\pi),\nu_{\Phi}(\bm a,\pi)\big),
 \label{eq:executor}
\end{equation}
where $\nu_{\Phi}$ is its routing-failure count. The fixed prioritised planner
defines an executor-relative feasible set. We report $\nu_{\Phi}$ separately
and evaluate makespan only on
$\mathcal F_{\Phi}=\{(\bm a,\pi):\nu_{\Phi}(\bm a,\pi)=0\}$. The objective is
\begin{equation}
 (\bm a^*,\pi^*)=
 \arg\min_{(\bm a,\pi)\in\mathcal F_{\Phi}}
 C_{\max}^{\Phi}(\bm a,\pi),
 \label{eq:objective}
\end{equation}
where $C_{\max}^{\Phi}=\max_k F_k$ and $F_k$ includes robot $k$'s return to
$b_k$. Failed training rollouts receive a penalty, but reported makespan and
failure count remain separate.

\subsection{Idealised Evaluation and Execution Gap}
\label{sec:penalty}

For robot $k$, let $\pi^k=(u_1^k,\ldots,u_{n_k}^k)$ be its operation
subsequence, set $\ell(u_0^k)=b_k$, and let
$\Delta_i^k=\Delta(\ell(u_{i-1}^k),\ell(u_i^k))$. The idealised evaluator
$\tilde\Phi$ ignores queues and inter-robot interference:
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \tilde C_k&=\sum_{i=1}^{n_k}\big(\Delta_i^k+p(u_i^k)\big)
 +\Delta\!\left(\ell(u_{n_k}^k),b_k\right),\\
 \tilde C&=\max_k\tilde C_k .
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:ideal}
\end{equation}
For the same executor-feasible schedule, its relative value error is
\begin{equation}
 \Lambda(\bm a,\pi)=
 \frac{C_{\max}^{\Phi}(\bm a,\pi)-\tilde C(\bm a,\pi)}
      {\tilde C(\bm a,\pi)}.
 \label{eq:penalty}
\end{equation}
We also record service waiting $Q_k$ and route delay $R_k$ beyond free-space
travel, with overlapping time counted only once. Their fleet-level ratios are
\begin{equation}
 \Omega_{\mathrm q}=\frac{\sum_kQ_k}{\sum_k\tilde C_k},\qquad
 \Omega_{\mathrm r}=\frac{\sum_kR_k}{\sum_k\tilde C_k}.
 \label{eq:operational-delays}
\end{equation}
These ratios diagnose robot-time inflation; they are neither independent
causal effects nor an additive decomposition of makespan. Section~\ref{sec:experiments}
also compares the rankings induced by $\tilde\Phi$ and $\Phi$.

\subsection{Constructive Decision Process}
\label{sec:mdp}

The schedule is built over $2n$ epochs. State and action are
\begin{align}
 s_t&=(\bm X_t^A,\bm X_t^J,\bm E_t,\pi_{1:t}),                \label{eq:state}\\
 a_t&=(\omega,k,j),\quad
 \omega\in\{\textsc{pick},\textsc{del}\},                    \label{eq:action}
\end{align}
where the features encode robot availability and rack occupancy, parcel
status, committed service windows, and the current prefix.
The action mask permits pickup only by a robot with compatible free capacity
and delivery only by the parcel's carrier. It thus guarantees assignment,
precedence, and rack feasibility; timing and routability are checked by
$\Phi$.

A calibrated surrogate $\hat\Psi$ supplies intermediate travel and waiting
features but not the terminal objective. Because a prefix has no unique
executor cost without a completion, training compares policy and baseline
prefixes completed by the same rule and evaluated by the same $\Phi$.
Section~\ref{sec:training} specifies this paired estimator.

\subsection{Assumptions}
\label{sec:assumptions}

Robots are homogeneous; all parcels are available at $t=0$; service times
are deterministic and class-dependent; and each service event handles one
parcel. Battery, turning and acceleration costs, localisation error, and
transshipment are excluded. Idle robots remain at non-blocking dedicated
bays. These assumptions delimit the controlled study; online arrivals and
executor generalisation remain outside its scope.
