\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Learned Constructive Dispatching}

Constructive deep-reinforcement-learning dispatchers originate with
L2D~\cite{zhang2020l2d}, which encodes the job-shop disjunctive graph with a
graph isomorphism network~\cite{xu2019gin}, commits one eligible operation
per decision epoch, and trains with a dense reward given by the change in a
makespan lower bound. Extensions to flexible job shops introduced
heterogeneous operation--machine graphs and composite
actions~\cite{song2023fjsp}; a recent survey traces the resulting family of
graph representations, architectures and training schemes~\cite{smit2024gnnsurvey}.

The transport-aware branch is well developed. Moon \emph{et
al.}~\cite{moon2024hgs} model operations, machines and vehicles as a
heterogeneous graph with composite actions; Yuan \emph{et
al.}~\cite{yuan2026gin} pair a graph isomorphism encoder with a hierarchical
action space and proximal policy optimisation; Yang \emph{et
al.}~\cite{yang2026cosched} add incremental graph updates for rescheduling;
and transformer encoders with dual-agent decompositions have been applied to
joint job and AGV dispatching~\cite{matrans2026}. Link \emph{et
al.}~\cite{link2026coordination} ask when joint training of the two
decisions is necessary at all, finding its advantage over modular training
narrows in bottleneck regimes.

We treat this body of work as settled and adopt its encoder and action
structure unmodified, claiming no novelty for that choice;
Section~\ref{sec:experiments} reports a controlled encoder comparison at
matched parameter count for precisely this reason. What distinguishes the
present work is the learning signal. The lower-bound-difference reward
of~\cite{zhang2020l2d} is available because an abstract machine model admits
consistent evaluation of a partial schedule; under a collision-aware
executor it does not, for the reason given in
Section~\ref{sec:consequences}.

\subsection{Scheduling with Transport}

Integrating material handling into shop scheduling dates to the simultaneous
machine-and-vehicle formulation of Bilge and Ulusoy~\cite{bilge1995time} and
the genetic-algorithm treatment that followed~\cite{ulusoy1997ga}, which
established that scheduling and vehicle assignment interact and must be
decided jointly, and supplied the instance family still used as a benchmark.

Across this line, classical and learned alike, transport is represented by a
fixed travel-time matrix and handover is instantaneous. The idealisation is
inherited from the founding formulations and has been carried forward
largely unexamined. Quantifying its cost, and establishing where that cost
is concentrated, is the empirical contribution of this paper.

\subsection{Conflict-Free Routing and Multi-Agent Path Finding}

A complementary literature represents motion exactly. Multi-agent path
finding computes collision-free space--time trajectories over shared
graphs~\cite{stern2019mapf}, and its lifelong pickup-and-delivery variant
serves online task streams in warehouse grids~\cite{ma2017lifelong};
capacitated agents and station queueing have both been treated within this
family, and learned components now appear inside the planner itself, for
instance by decoding priority orders for prioritised planning from an
attention network~\cite{rlrhpp2026}. These methods model the transport layer
faithfully, but assume the task assignment as given and are posed as online
service of an arriving stream rather than offline construction of a complete
schedule.

Closest to our premise is Noda~\cite{noda2026deferral}, who observes in the
deadline-constrained MAPD setting that immediate commitment can degrade
downstream service through congestion and reservation interactions, and
proposes a one-parameter deferral gate over token-passing baselines. That
work and ours begin from the same observation and diverge in what follows:
\cite{noda2026deferral} exploits the interaction online through a tuned
threshold, whereas we measure its magnitude and composition and use them to
correct the training signal of an offline constructive policy.

A further line couples scheduling with conflict-free routing directly, by
local and random search for capacitated AGV
systems~\cite{miyamoto2016local}, by integrated scheduling in container
terminals~\cite{zhong2020integrated}, by a hierarchical framework placing a
reinforcement-learning assignment layer above a conflict-free path
generator~\cite{li2024hierarchical}, and by time-space network models for
just-in-time delivery~\cite{nishida2023jit}. Two properties separate this
group from the present work. First, routing feasibility is enforced per
instance by search or mathematical programming, so no reusable policy is
produced; the hierarchical scheme of~\cite{li2024hierarchical} learns an
assignment layer, but trains it against a separate routing stage rather than
against the realised schedule. Second, and more consequentially, service
points are taken to be uncontended: loading and unloading begin on arrival,
so no queue forms at a pickup or delivery node~\cite{nishida2023jit}.
Waiting is thus represented where vehicles meet in transit and omitted where
they meet at a server, which Section~\ref{sec:experiments} identifies as the
larger of the two effects.

\subsection{Credit Assignment without an Intermediate Reward}

When an objective is available only on complete solutions, policy-gradient
methods~\cite{williams1992reinforce} require a baseline computed by rollout.
Self-critical sequence training compares a sampled trajectory against a
greedily decoded one~\cite{rennie2017self}, and the attention model of Kool
\emph{et al.}~\cite{kool2019attention} applies the same device to routing
problems using a deterministic rollout of a frozen policy. Our estimator
belongs to this family and differs in two respects: the baseline completes
each \emph{prefix} with a dispatching rule rather than completing only the
initial state, yielding a per-decision rather than per-episode advantage;
and both branches are evaluated by the collision-aware executor rather than
by the model on which the policy acts. The novelty claimed is the grounding,
not the rollout. Auxiliary shaping terms are used with care, since shaping
alters the optimal policy unless it takes potential form~\cite{ng1999shaping};
the corresponding ablation appears in Section~\ref{sec:experiments}.

\subsection{Position of This Work}

The problem-side literatures each retain a different portion of the realised
transfer time: learned dispatching retains travel; integrated conflict-free
routing retains travel and routing excess; multi-agent path finding retains
both but fixes the assignment. None represents queueing at capacitated,
exclusive service points \emph{and} learns a reusable constructive policy
against the router that will execute it. This paper occupies that
intersection. Its contribution is not the combination, nor the architecture,
but the measurement that motivates the combination: the omitted terms are
large, they grow with fleet density, and they are dominated by the component
the scheduling decision determines.
