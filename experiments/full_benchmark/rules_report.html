Batching buys a shorter plan and pays for it in door queues

milk_run holds parcels on the rack until it has a full load, which cuts travel: at n = 100 its ideal is 409.0 against the completion family's 484.0, a 75-unit better plan. It then hands back most of a 23% gap to queueing — Ωq of 0.30 against 0.04, seven times the door contention.

Whether that trade pays depends entirely on how much travel there is to save. At n = 20 there is almost none, so the queueing is pure loss and milk_run finishes 19 units behind. By n = 100 the saved travel outweighs the queues and it wins by 31. The crossover lands at n = 60, where the two are statistically indistinguishable — a paired difference of 2.4 against a confidence interval of 6.5.

This also explains the confidence intervals. milk_run carries ±6.5 to ±8.5 throughout while the completion family sits at ±3.5 to ±4.9: queueing outcomes depend on which robots happen to converge on a door, so batching is the higher-variance strategy as well as the higher-ceiling one.

A good job rule makes the robot-selection rule irrelevant

Pair earliest_completion_job with any of the six AMR rules — including random — and the result moves by under 1%. Pair fifo with them and the result moves by 144%. The job rule decides which parcels get batched and when doors are hit; once that is right there is little left for the robot choice to spoil, and once it is wrong no robot choice can rescue it.

Practical consequence: tuning the AMR rule is not worth the search. Six of the sixty combinations are just milk_run paired with six different AMR rules, and they occupy the entire top six at n = 100 within a 10-unit band.

Stability

Seven combinations never leave the top 15

Of sixty, these seven place in the top fifteen at every parcel count from 20 to 100. All are from the completion family — none is ever the outright winner at n = 80 or 100, but none ever collapses either.

  • earliest_completion_job + material_match — the outright best at n = 20 and n = 40
  • earliest_completion_job + earliest_completion
  • earliest_completion_job + nearest_amr
  • earliest_completion_job + random
  • material_match + earliest_completion
  • material_match + material_match
  • material_match + random

If a single rule has to be fixed across an unknown workload, one of these is the defensible choice. If the workload is known and large, milk_run is worth 31 units at n = 100 — but it is the wrong default below n = 60.

Note that milk_run+earliest_completion is the baseline the policies were trained against. It ranks 3rd at n = 60, 1st at n = 80, and 3rd at n = 100 — a strong counterfactual at the sizes that matter, and a weak one at n = 20 where it does not place at all.

Worst

What the spread costs

The gap between the best and worst combination widens from 2.5× at n = 20 to 4.1× at n = 100. least_congested_station+random is the worst at every size, reaching 2073.1 against the best 501.5 — choosing the rule badly costs more than every method difference in this benchmark put together.

Method

How to read these tables

  • Instances. test_case/v3/trend/full_{20,40,60,80,100}.jsonl, 100 per size, m = 16. Rules are deterministic given seed 42, so each combination is one schedule per instance.
  • Scoring. Each schedule is built under the calibrated surrogate and scored once by the collision-aware executor. Makespan, ideal, gap, and Ωq are averaged over routable instances only; a ν marker flags any combination with unroutable instances (never more than one).
  • Confidence intervals. 1.96 × sd / √n across instances. Head-to-head deltas are paired within instance, which is why they resolve differences the overlapping intervals alone would not.
  • Timing. Seconds per instance from a single-process pass on an idle machine, not from the parallel sweep.
