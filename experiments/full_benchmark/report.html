The training return is the whole story

The A/B/C/D cells cross two factors: whether the congestion channels are masked during training, and whether the return is the idealised makespan or the executor's. Cell D is v9's only60_s44. One factor dominates and the two interact sharply.

Training on the idealised return costs far more than masking congestion

At n = 60, swapping the return from idealised to executor is worth 318 units when the mask is on (A → C) and 65 units when it is off (B → D). Masking the congestion channels costs only 11 units under an executor return (D → C) but 264 units under an idealised one (B → A).

The two factors are not additive. A policy trained on a return that cannot see congestion and denied the features that would let it infer congestion degrades catastrophically, and the damage widens with workload — cell A is nearly twice cell D by n = 100.

One thing to check: v10_B_s44_best.pth and v10_B_s44_bestideal.pth contain byte-identical weight tensors, so cell B's two selection criteria landed on the same epoch. The files differ only in metadata. Cell A's two selections are genuinely different, and the C̃-selected checkpoint is the better of them at every size — 945.0 vs 1042.6 at n = 100.

Dispatching rules

Which rule, and how much the choice matters

The ranking moves with workload. Below n = 60 the completion-time and material-matching job rules lead; from n = 60 up, milk_run takes over as door contention starts to dominate. The AMR rule matters far less than the job rule — the top six at n = 100 are all milk_run paired with six different AMR rules.

Execution gap

Ideal versus real

The gap is the distance between the idealised decode — free-space travel, service on arrival, no interference — and what the executor actually delivers. It is a diagnostic, not the objective: a method that changes the assignment changes the ideal too, so it can lower its gap percentage by inflating the denominator. That is exactly what happens here.

The policy earns a better plan and gives part of it back to congestion

Greedy finds the lowest ideal of any method at n = 60 — 263.1 against the rule's 282.8 and the GA's 297.8 — meaning it assigns parcels to robots better than anything else here. It then surrenders 75.8 units to queueing and routing, more than the rule's 64.0, and finishes in a near-tie.

The GA sits at the opposite corner: the worst assignment, the mildest congestion. Its Ωq of 0.087 is a quarter of the rule's, because the collision-free surrogate it optimises against still enforces exclusive doors, so it learns to stagger arrivals even though it cannot see collisions.

Best-of-8 is the only method that gets both: it gives up a little ideal relative to greedy and recovers far more in execution, which is what an executor-scored selection is supposed to do.

Method

How this was run

  • Instances. test_case/v3/trend/full_{20,40,60,80,100}.jsonl, 100 per size. Verified zero overlap against train_60, test_60, and every train_mix_n / val_mix_n pool — so clean for v9 and v10 alike. There are no held-out test sets at 20/40/80/100 other than these; val_mix is the checkpoint-selection set and could not be used.
  • Inference. The standard pairing throughout: plan under the calibrated surrogate, score once with the collision-aware executor. Not the executor-in-the-loop variant.
  • Masks. v10 cells A and C were trained with the L3 congestion mask installed and are evaluated with it installed. Cells B and D and all of v9 run unmasked. The mask zeroes amr[9], dock[2:7], and action[2]; this was verified against live feature tensors before the sweep.
  • Averaging. Makespan, ideal, and gap are averaged over routable instances only (ν = 0). Unroutable counts are kept separately: 40 of 30,000 rule schedules, 39 of 5,500 greedy, none for best-of-8 or GA.
  • Excluded. _latest checkpoints (end-of-training, not selected) and the GA's collision-aware search arm, which is far slower per evaluation and would have dominated the compute budget.
