# Val-selected baseline for the full benchmark

## Why this stage exists

`summarise.py:57` builds the headline with `min(g, key=lambda r: r["makespan_mean"])`,
applied separately to the rule family and to the policy family, **on the reporting set**.
Both halves of the comparison are therefore optimistic order statistics rather than scores
any practitioner could have obtained in advance:

- the rule row is a minimum over 60 job-rule × AMR-rule combinations on `trend/full_{n}`;
- the policy row is a minimum over seeds on the same instances — and the winning seed
  changes with `n` (s42 at 20/60, s44 at 40, s43 at 80/100 in `summary.txt`), which is what
  noise-driven selection looks like.

At n=60 in `experiments/policy_vs_rules`, taking the best of three seeds on test is worth
8.2 makespan units, against a claimed effect of 6.94 units versus the strongest test rule.
The selection is larger than the finding.

This stage moves both choices onto a separate split and reports what the frozen choice
scores on the reporting set.

## Splits

| split | files | instances/size | role |
|---|---|---|---|
| train | `test_case/v3/mix/train_mix_{n}.jsonl`, `test_case/v3/train_60.jsonl` | 5000 / varies | policy training |
| **val** | `test_case/v3/mix/val_mix_{n}.jsonl` | 40 | **all selection** |
| test | `test_case/v3/trend/full_{n}.jsonl` | 100 | reporting only |

`n ∈ {20, 40, 60, 80, 100}`, m = 16.

`val_mix` is not a new set: it is already the validation set every v9 and v10 run was
scored against during training (`launch_v9.sh:23`, `launch_v10_2x3.sh:46`). No dispatching
rule has ever seen it. `run_val_selection.sh` refuses to proceed unless the three families
of file are disjoint, checked by hashing each instance's job list — "generated separately"
is not a proof of disjointness.

## What is selected on val, per size

1. **Dispatching rule** — `argmin` over all 60 combinations of mean executed makespan on
   `val_mix_{n}`, with a 0.5-unit tie band broken alphabetically. Selected independently at
   each `n`, because the rule field reorders with problem size: `milk_run` variants lead at
   n=60 but are far off the pace at n=20.
2. **Seed** — `argmin` within a recipe (`v9_only60`, `v9_mix`). Reported next to the
   between-seed sd so a pick made inside the noise band is visible as one.
3. **Model** — `argmin` across all checkpoints, per decoding mode.

`selection_val.json` is the frozen output. Val scores appear in it and in
`selection_val_summary.txt` and are **not results**: each `_best.pth` is itself an argmin
over many `val_mix` evaluations, so a policy's val score is an optimistic order statistic on
exactly this set. That is what a validation set is for — ranking candidates — but it makes
the number useless as a generalisation estimate.

## What is reported on test

Three rows per (size, decoding mode), kept apart because they answer different questions:

| row | policy side | rule side | question |
|---|---|---|---|
| **PRIMARY** | seed-averaged recipe | val-selected | how good is this training recipe against a rule chosen in advance |
| **DEPLOYED** | val-selected seed | val-selected | what would actually have shipped |
| ORACLE | best seed on test | best rule on test | what `summarise.py` currently prints — shown as the bias it is |

Seeds are averaged *within* instance before differencing; 3 seeds × 100 instances are three
correlated measurements of each instance, not 300 samples. Pairing is asserted on the
instance id, only instances both sides routed cleanly (`nu == 0`) enter a mean, and each
per-instance delta set gets a normal CI, a 10 000-resample percentile bootstrap CI,
win/tie/loss counts, and a 10 000-flip paired permutation test — the conventions in
`experiments/policy_vs_rules/analyze_policy.py`.

Two diagnostics accompany the ORACLE row, both in makespan units and both directly
comparable to the PRIMARY delta they would otherwise have inflated:

- `rule_selection_regret` — what the val-chosen rule costs versus the best rule on test.
- `seed_cherry_pick_gain` — what picking the best seed on test buys over the seed average.

| file | contents |

new headlines are byte-identical and the two tables differ only in which rows they select.

## Findings

Run on 2026-08-25. Selection sweep: 16 400 rows (5 sizes × (60 rules + 11 checkpoints × 2
modes) × 40 instances). The reporting sweep was not touched.

### Every verdict survives the protocol change

|---:|:--|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|:--|

| 40 | greedy | 257.7 | 254.1 | −3.65 | 258.4 | 255.4 | **−3.02** | policy (unchanged) |
| 60 | greedy | 347.1 | 338.9 | −8.20 | 349.8 | 341.5 | **−8.21** | policy (unchanged) |
| 80 | greedy | 426.2 | 432.3 | +6.03 | 426.2 | 435.3 | **+9.07** | rule (unchanged) |
| 100 | greedy | 501.5 | 521.8 | +20.30 | 506.0 | 527.3 | **+21.30** | rule (unchanged) |
| 20 | best8 | 166.2 | 160.2 | −6.02 | 166.2 | 160.2 | **−6.04** | policy (unchanged) |
| 40 | best8 | 257.7 | 242.2 | −15.59 | 258.4 | 242.9 | **−15.45** | policy (unchanged) |

| 80 | best8 | 426.2 | 412.0 | −14.22 | 426.2 | 414.0 | **−12.26** | policy (unchanged) |
| 100 | best8 | 501.5 | 496.6 | −4.92 | 506.0 | 500.6 | **−5.37** | policy, **now n.s.** (p = 0.116) |

OLD is `summarise.py`'s headline (both sides `argmin` on test); NEW is PRIMARY (both sides
chosen on val, policy seed-averaged). Nine of ten cells keep their sign, their significance
and roughly their magnitude. The one substantive change is n=100 best8: the −4.92 the old
table reports comes from one lucky seed, and the recipe average −5.37 carries a CI that
crosses zero.

The headline conclusion is therefore not an artifact of picking on the reporting set:
**greedy decoding beats a rule chosen in advance only at n ∈ {40, 60}** — at and just below
the size it was trained on — while **best-of-8 beats it at every size, significantly up to
n = 80.**

### The rule field is a plateau, so choosing in advance costs almost nothing

Val picked a different combination from the test oracle at four of five sizes, and it barely
mattered:

| n | val-selected rule | on test | best rule on test | regret |
|---:|:--|---:|:--|---:|
| 20 | `material_match+material_match` | 166.2 | `earliest_completion_job+material_match` | 0.02 |
| 40 | `earliest_completion_job+earliest_completion` | 258.4 | `earliest_completion_job+material_match` | 0.64 |
| 60 | `earliest_completion_job+earliest_completion` | 349.6 | `milk_run+least_loaded` | 2.74 |
| 80 | `milk_run+earliest_completion` | 426.2 | `milk_run+earliest_completion` | 0.00 |
| 100 | `milk_run+material_match` | 506.0 | `milk_run+earliest_available` | 4.48 |

This table is unpaired — each rule averaged over the instances *it* routed — so a cell can
differ by a few tenths from the `rule_mean` printed in a PRIMARY row, which averages over the
paired subset both sides routed (e.g. 349.6 here vs 349.8 at n=60 greedy, where one instance
drops). Median regret 0.64 units, worst 4.48 (0.9% at n=100). Consistent with
`experiments/congestion_penalty/final_selection_regret.csv`, which measures 0.44% at m=16
from an independent direction. The baseline is not weakened by being chosen honestly, which
is the fact that makes the honest protocol cheap to adopt.

Note the rule that wins *reorders* with size — `milk_run` leads at n ≥ 80 and is off the pace
at n=20 — so the selection has to be made per size. A single rule fixed across the sweep
would be a strawman at one end or the other.

### The seed cherry-pick was the larger bias, not the rule pick

Counting candidates suggests the opposite (60 rules versus 3 seeds), but the measurement
disagrees, because the top of the rule field is flat while seeds are spread:

| n | rule selection regret | seed cherry-pick gain |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 20 | 0.02 | 2.59 |
| 40 | 0.64 | 1.28 |
| 60 | 2.88 | 2.64 |
| 80 | 0.01 | 3.05 |
| 100 | 4.48 | 5.48 |

(greedy; both in makespan units, both "what picking on test buys that side")

At n=20 and n=80 the old headline flattered the *policy* by 2.6 and 3.1 units against a rule
pick that was worth ~0. Best-of-3-seeds is worth 1.3–5.5 units across the sweep, against
PRIMARY effects of 3.0–21.3 — so at the sizes where the effect is smallest, it is the same
order as the effect.

### Limitations

- 40 val instances per size is thin for ranking 60 rule combinations. It is enough here only
  because the top of the field is a plateau; the regret column is the evidence for that, and
  it should be re-checked if the facility geometry changes. `generate_instances_v3.py
  --jobs N --count 100 --seed <fresh>` widens it if needed.
- v10 cells contribute one seed (s44) each, so nothing is averaged for them; `v10_C_s44`
  wins n=20 best8 on val as a single draw and its PRIMARY row is that same draw.
- The GA is not in the selection sweep. It has no hyperparameter being chosen here, and its
  test rows are unaffected.

## Variant: every recipe held at one seed (`compare_fixed_seed.py`)

The v10 cells were only ever trained at s44, so a 3-seed v9 average sitting next to a 1-seed
v10 number compares a recipe mean against a single draw. `compare_fixed_seed.py --seed s44`
holds every row at the same seed so they are the same kind of object. The rule baseline is
unchanged — still the combination frozen on val.

Fixing a seed is defensible here only because s44 is the seed v10 *has*, not the seed that
looks good. The script checks rather than assumes this, and prints where s44 ranked among
its recipe's seeds on val:

| mode | n=20 | n=40 | n=60 | n=80 | n=100 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| greedy | 2/3 | 3/3 | 2/3 | 3/3 | 3/3 |
| best8 | 1/3 | 1/3 | 2/3 | 3/3 | 3/3 |

s44 is the *worst* val seed at five of ten cells and the best at two, so this is a matching
constraint, not a pick in the policy's favour. Had it been val-best everywhere the script
says so and the framing would have to change to "seed selection".

Eight of ten verdicts are identical. Two soften, both in the same direction — s44 is a
below-median seed at large `n`, so the shift is positive (worse for the policy) at every

- **n=20 greedy** stops being a rule win and becomes a tie (p = 0.15).
- **n=100 best8** goes from a marginal −5.37 to −1.68 at p = 0.64, i.e. an unambiguous wash
  rather than a near-miss. The n=100 best8 advantage does not survive at a single seed.

Nothing here changes the shape of the result: greedy holds up only near its training size,
best8 wins from n=20 to n=80 and runs out at n=100.

| `v9_mix_s44` | +15.6 | +33.7 | +92.9 |
| `v10_B_s44` | +69.9 | +58.0 | +102.0 |
| `v10_A_s44` | +30.6 | +321.8 | +537.4 |

`v10_C` is the only v10 cell in contention, and it beats `v9_only60_s44` at exactly one cell

**How many seeds independently support a significant policy win:**

| n | greedy | best8 |
|---:|:--|:--|
| 20 | 0/3 | **3/3** |
| 40 | **1/3 — fragile** | **3/3** |
| 60 | 3/3 | **3/3** |
| 80 | 0/3 | **3/3** |
| 100 | 0/3 | **1/3 — fragile** |

Two cells rest on a single seed and should be reported as such:

- **n=40 greedy.** The 3-seed average (−3.02, p = 0.047) reads as a win, but only s44
  reaches significance on its own; s43 is −0.85 at p = 0.63. This cell is a tie dressed up
  by averaging.
- **n=100 best8.** Only s43 wins (−9.40, p = 0.009); s42 and s44 are −5.04 and −1.68, both
  n.s. s43 is also the val pick here, so the DEPLOYED number is real — but it is one draw,
  and the recipe does not beat the rule at n=100.

Everything else is unanimous across seeds in both directions. The claim that survives all
three seeds independently is: **best-of-8 beats a rule chosen in advance at n = 20–80;
greedy beats it only at n = 60; and at n = 100 neither decoding mode does.**

**Sampling suppresses seed variance where it works.** At n ≤ 60 the best8 spread is
0.8–0.9 makespan units against 3.7–5.5 for greedy — taking the best of 8 rollouts washes out
most of what separates the seeds. The suppression fails exactly where the method starts
failing: spread returns to 4.5 at n=80 and 7.7 at n=100.

**`v9_mix` loses everywhere, at every seed.** Delta versus the val-selected rule is positive
in all 30 cells except one tie (n=20 best8 s42, +0.91 at p = 0.45), reaching +92.9 at n=100
greedy. Its between-seed spread is also 2–5× worse than `v9_only60`'s (up to 24.1 units at
n=40 best8). Training across sizes made the recipe both weaker and less stable than training
at n=60 alone — a clean negative result, and unambiguous because it does not depend on which
seed is reported.
